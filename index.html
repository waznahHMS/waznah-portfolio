04 RESEARCH & AI GOVERNANCE

Research & Policy Work

Exploring the environmental and governance dimensions of AI infrastructure in Southeast Asia.

Capstone — Distinction Award Waznah — Outstanding Fellow

Governing AI's Hidden Footprint: Data Centre Environmental Disclosure in Brunei and Malaysia toward an ASEAN Framework

AI for Asia Fellowship 2026 Capstone · Team 7_Elevan

This capstone project examines the environmental impact of AI infrastructure, focusing on data centre energy consumption and environmental disclosure practices in Brunei and Malaysia. It proposes policy recommendations toward an ASEAN-level governance framework for responsible AI infrastructure. My contribution particularly highlighted the Brunei-focused research.

What the paper is about

As AI adoption accelerates across Southeast Asia, the physical infrastructure behind it — data centres — has quietly become one of the region's fastest-growing sources of energy demand and environmental impact. Yet disclosure and governance frameworks have not kept pace.

Our paper asks: how can Brunei and Malaysia move toward transparent, comparable environmental disclosure for AI-related data centre infrastructure — and what would an ASEAN-level framework look like?

Key questions we explored
  • What do current data centre environmental disclosure practices look like in Brunei and Malaysia?
  • Where are the gaps in existing regulation and reporting standards?
  • How does AI-driven demand shape energy consumption patterns in the region?
  • What could an ASEAN-level governance framework for responsible AI infrastructure include?
Methodology
  • Comparative policy and regulatory review across Brunei and Malaysia
  • Qualitative analysis of disclosure practices and governance frameworks
  • Review of existing regional and international environmental reporting standards
  • Synthesis of policy recommendations toward an ASEAN framework
What we produced
  • A comparative analysis of Brunei's and Malaysia's current disclosure landscape
  • Policy recommendations for strengthening environmental transparency in AI infrastructure
  • A proposed direction for an ASEAN-level governance framework for responsible AI infrastructure
Why it matters

AI's environmental footprint is often invisible in policy conversations. This paper contributes to the emerging conversation on responsible AI infrastructure — connecting technology, climate, and regional governance across Southeast Asia.

My contribution: I focused on the Brunei side of the research — covering Brunei's current disclosure landscape, its position within ASEAN, and how it might engage with a future regional framework. The capstone received a Distinction Award under the AI for Asia Fellowship 2026, and I was personally recognised as an Outstanding Fellow.

Note: This summary is for informational purposes. Publication status and policy adoption are not claimed. Full paper is not publicly available at this time.
